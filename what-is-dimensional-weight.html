What Is Dimensional Weight (DIM Weight)?

How UPS and FedEx calculate DIM weight, when it applies, worked examples, and 5 ways to reduce your DIM charges.

What Is Dimensional Weight and Why Does It Exist?

Dimensional weight — also called DIM weight, volumetric weight, or cubed weight — is a pricing method used by UPS, FedEx, and most other major carriers that charges you based on a package's volume, not just its physical weight. The idea is straightforward: a carrier's trucks and aircraft have a fixed amount of space. A lightweight package that takes up a large amount of that space costs the carrier real money in capacity, even if the package itself barely registers on a scale.

Before 2015, DIM weight pricing for domestic UPS and FedEx shipments only applied to packages above a certain size threshold. Starting January 1, 2015, both carriers eliminated the threshold — DIM weight pricing now applies to virtually all domestic packages. This was a major shift that significantly increased shipping costs for businesses with "light but large" product profiles: think pillows, pet beds, foam packaging, balloons, large clothing items, or anything shipped in oversized cartons.

In practice, DIM weight pricing means you might pay for 22 lbs of shipping weight on a package that physically weighs 4 lbs — if the box is large enough. Understanding how this calculation works and how to manage it is essential for any business that ships product.

The DIM Weight Formula

(L × W × H) ÷ DIM Factor = DIM Weight
Measure in inches. Round up to the next whole pound. Use whichever is greater: DIM weight or actual weight.

The DIM Factor is a constant divisor set by the carrier. A higher DIM Factor produces a lower DIM weight — which is better for the shipper. Here are the current standard DIM Factors:

166

Note: Some negotiated carrier agreements include a higher DIM Factor (166 or even 194 domestically) for large-volume shippers. This is one of the highest-value terms to negotiate in your carrier contract.

Worked Example: A 12×12×12 Box

Let's walk through a real calculation. Suppose you're shipping a lightweight product in a standard 12×12×12-inch box, and the packed box weighs 5 lbs.

Step-by-step calculation
Package dimensions12" × 12" × 12"
Step 1: Multiply L × W × H12 × 12 × 12 = 1,728 cubic inches
Step 2: Divide by DIM Factor (139)1,728 ÷ 139 = 12.43 lbs
Step 3: Round up to next whole poundDIM Weight = 13 lbs
Actual package weight5 lbs
Billable weight (greater of the two)13 lbs — you pay for 13 lbs, not 5!

At a Zone 5 UPS Ground rate of roughly $0.90/lb after discounts, that's $11.70 billed vs. $4.50 if actual weight were used — a 160% premium caused by the box size. Multiply this across thousands of packages per month and the impact on shipping cost is dramatic.

A Larger Example: The 18×14×12 Box

Package dimensions18" × 14" × 12"
Volume18 × 14 × 12 = 3,024 cubic inches
DIM Weight (÷ 139)3,024 ÷ 139 = 21.75 → 22 lbs
Actual weight4 lbs
Billable weight22 lbs

This is a common scenario for businesses shipping apparel, bedding, stuffed animals, or any product with significant air space in the packaging. If your DIM factor were negotiated to 166, the same box would produce a DIM weight of (3,024 ÷ 166) = 18.2 → 19 lbs — still higher than actual, but 3 lbs lower than at 139, saving roughly $2.70 per package on a per-pound rate of $0.90.

When Does Dimensional Weight Apply?

UPS and FedEx apply DIM weight pricing when the calculated DIM weight exceeds the package's actual weight. The carrier then bills the higher of the two numbers as your billable weight.

  • If actual weight (7 lbs) > DIM weight (5 lbs) → you pay for 7 lbs
  • If DIM weight (12 lbs) > actual weight (3 lbs) → you pay for 12 lbs

Both UPS and FedEx apply DIM pricing to ground and express domestic services. International services use the 166 DIM factor by default. The calculation happens automatically when you print a label — carriers also have dimensioning equipment at their facilities that can capture measurements and apply DIM weight retroactively as an adjustment if the declared dimensions were incorrect.

⚠️ Carrier Dimensioning Equipment

Modern UPS and FedEx facilities use automated dimensioning cameras and scales that capture package measurements as they move through sorting. If your declared dimensions differ from measured dimensions, a DIM weight adjustment can appear on a future invoice — weeks after shipping. Accurate label dimensions are essential to avoid adjustment chargebacks.

How to Calculate DIM Weight for Your Packages

Follow these steps for each package type in your product catalog:

1
Measure accurately — outside dimensions.

Use a tape measure on the longest side (Length), the next longest (Width), and the shortest (Height) of the outer carton — not the product itself. Round each measurement up to the nearest inch.

2
Multiply L × W × H.

This gives you the cubic inch volume of the package.

3
Divide by your DIM Factor (139 for standard, or your negotiated factor).

This gives you the DIM weight in pounds.

4
Round up to the next whole pound.

13.2 lbs DIM weight bills as 14 lbs.

5
Compare to actual weight — use the larger number.

This is your billable weight for rate calculation purposes.

5 Ways to Reduce Your DIM Weight Charges

1
Right-size your packaging.

The most direct way to reduce DIM weight is to use a smaller box. Work with a packaging engineer or your box supplier to find the optimal carton size for each of your most common order types. Even reducing a box by 2 inches on one dimension can eliminate DIM weight entirely on lightweight products. This is the highest-ROI DIM reduction strategy for most businesses.

2
Eliminate void fill, don't just manage it.

Shippers who use peanuts, air pillows, or paper fill to cushion products in oversized boxes are paying twice: once for the fill material, and again in DIM weight. Re-engineering packaging to minimize void space reduces DIM weight while also cutting material costs.

3
Negotiate a higher DIM Factor in your carrier contract.

For high-volume shippers, negotiating a DIM factor of 166 (instead of the standard 139) reduces every DIM-affected package's billable weight by approximately 15%. This is a significant savings driver that compounds across your entire DIM-affected package volume — and it's negotiable for businesses shipping above certain monthly thresholds. Learn more about contract negotiation →

4
Use flat-rate or regional rate packaging where available.

USPS Priority Mail flat-rate boxes do not use DIM weight pricing — you pay based on the flat rate, regardless of what's inside (within weight limits). For certain product types and zone distances, USPS flat-rate boxes can significantly undercut DIM-billed UPS/FedEx rates. Regional rate packaging offers similar benefits at shorter zone distances.

5
Zone skipping for high-DIM-weight product lines.

Zone skipping — consolidating packages and injecting them into the carrier network at a facility closer to the destination — reduces the zone-based cost of shipping, which directly reduces the dollar impact of DIM weight since DIM weight interacts with zone-based rate tables. For businesses with concentrated customer geographies, zone skipping combined with right-sized packaging can deliver compounding savings on DIM-heavy SKUs.

DIM Weight FAQ

Q: What is dimensional weight?
Dimensional weight (DIM weight) is a calculated shipping weight based on a package's volume rather than its physical weight. Carriers use DIM weight to ensure they're compensated when a light package occupies significant space on a truck or aircraft. You pay whichever is greater: actual weight or DIM weight.
Q: How do I calculate dimensional weight for my packages?
Use this formula: (Length × Width × Height in inches) ÷ DIM Factor = DIM Weight. For standard domestic UPS and FedEx shipments, the DIM Factor is 139. Round the result up to the next whole pound. If your DIM weight exceeds your actual package weight, you'll be billed at DIM weight.
Q: What is the DIM factor for UPS and FedEx?
The standard DIM factor for domestic US shipments is 139 for both UPS and FedEx. For international shipments, the standard factor is 166. High-volume shippers can negotiate a higher domestic DIM factor (such as 166 or 194) in their carrier contracts, which reduces the DIM weight calculated for every qualifying package.
Q: When does dimensional weight apply?
Dimensional weight applies to virtually all domestic UPS and FedEx parcel services as of January 2015. It applies whenever your calculated DIM weight exceeds your package's actual physical weight. Both UPS and FedEx also measure packages with automated dimensioning equipment at their facilities — if your label dimensions are inaccurate, you may receive a DIM weight adjustment on a future invoice.
Q: Can I negotiate my DIM factor?
Yes. Negotiating a higher DIM factor (for example, 166 instead of 139) in your carrier contract is one of the highest-ROI terms available to volume shippers with light-but-large product profiles. A higher DIM factor reduces the calculated DIM weight on every affected package, lowering your effective per-package cost.
